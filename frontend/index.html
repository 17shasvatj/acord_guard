This layer keeps AI-generated documents grounded in fact.

A certificate of insurance is a legal attestation people rely on. One wrong value — a date, a limit, a checked box — is a liability the issuing agency owns. Below: a real client file, one real example of what can go wrong, and a verification layer you can run yourself, live.

STEP 1 — BACKGROUND The situation, and the documents the AI is given to work from.

The client file

An insurance agency (Bayline Insurance Services) generating a certificate for a commercial customer. Everything the AI is allowed to use comes from the customer's policy you upload, the request it's answering, and the agency's own settings — the three sources below. Nothing else.

The customer's insurance policy (the “declarations page”)

The official summary of a business's coverage: which protections they carry, the dollar limits (the most the insurer will pay), and from when until when the coverage runs. You upload this as a PDF below — the layer reads it for every value it puts on the certificate, including whether the policy carries the endorsements needed to extend coverage to anyone else.

A certificate request

A general contractor won't let the business work on its site without proof of insurance. It asks for a certificate — and wants to be named as “additional insured” with a “waiver of subrogation,” two legal protections that only exist if the policy actually carries the matching endorsements. Whether the uploaded policy does is exactly what the layer checks.

The agency's own settings

The issuing agency's fixed details — its name, address, phone, and producer code — that belong on every certificate it puts out. These come from the agency's account settings, not from the customer's file, and the layer treats them as a source like any other.

A certificate of insurance is one page an insured business hands to a client or landlord as proof of coverage. Whoever receives it relies on the dollar amounts, the dates, and the checked boxes to be true — they let the business onto the job on the strength of that paper. If a value is wrong, the party relying on it is exposed, and the agency that issued it is liable.

STEP 2 — WHY GROUNDING MATTERS The structural gap in AI-generated documents.

Nothing guarantees that a generated value came from the policy

A language model generates plausible documents; it doesn't guarantee that every value traces to the source file, that missing information stays blank instead of being filled from training memory, or that the finished form passes the checks a human desk would run. Most runs come out fine. But insurance is an expensive-mistake industry — a certificate is relied on, litigated over, and owned by the agency that issued it. "Usually fine" isn't a control.

THE PRINCIPLE

The model proposes. Code disposes.

The layer below makes grounding structural. The model is not allowed to write on the document — it may only propose a value together with the exact sentence from the source that supports it. Deterministic code checks the sentence is really there and really contains the value, runs the desk checks, and drops anything that fails. A value with no source has nothing to quote, so it cannot reach the certificate — and every value that does carries a receipt: which document, which sentence.

STEP 3 — THE DEMO The verification layer, running live. This is the part to try.

Run the layer yourself — live

The model may only propose a value with the exact sentence from the source that supports it; deterministic code verifies the quote, runs the checks a human desk would run, and makes the final call. The layer approves a certificate when the policy backs everything on it — warns where a desk would want a second look — like a policy whose coverage period has ended — and stops what should never ship: unsupported values forced straight into the checker. Every run ends with a finished document and a receipt for every value on it.

The model reads your policy for the coverage dates, limits, carrier, and — crucially — whether the endorsements exist to support the requested boxes. The certificate request stays fixed (it asks for additional-insured and waiver-of-subrogation status). If the PDF is a scan with no text layer, you'll see every field come back “not found” — that's the layer refusing to guess on something it can't read, not a bug.
